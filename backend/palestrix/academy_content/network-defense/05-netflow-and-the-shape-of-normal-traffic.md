# NetFlow and the shape of normal traffic

You cannot store full packet capture for a busy network for more than a few
days. You can store flow records for years, and flow records answer most of
the questions you will actually ask during an investigation.

## What a flow record is

A summary of one conversation, emitted by a router or switch:

```
start                 duration  proto  src:port           dst:port          packets  bytes  flags
2026-03-12 09:18:07   0.412     TCP    10.24.1.42:51203   45.83.201.14:443  12       1840   .AP.SF
```

No payload. Ten fields. A few dozen bytes per conversation instead of every
packet, which is why a year of flow data fits where a week of capture does
not.

## The questions it answers well

- Did this host ever talk to that address, and when? (Retrospective hunting,
  months back, is the killer feature.)
- Who else in the estate talked to it?
- How much data left, and when?
- What is this host's normal set of peers?
- Which internal hosts talk to each other, and did that change?

## The questions it cannot answer

- What was actually sent. No payload, ever.
- Which URL, which filename, which credential.
- Whether a TLS session carried a file or a heartbeat — though size and timing
  imply a great deal.

Flow tells you *that* and *how much*. Capture and logs tell you *what*. Start
with flow, then pull the capture or the proxy log for the one window that
matters.

## Baselining: what "normal" means concretely

"Anomaly detection" is only useful if you can say what the norm is. Build
these, per host or per role, and most detections fall out of them:

- **Peer set.** Which addresses this host talks to in a week. Workstations
  have a large, changing set; servers have a small, stable one. A server
  contacting a new external address is a much stronger signal than a laptop
  doing the same.
- **Port profile.** A database server that has only ever spoken 5432 and 22
  suddenly answering on 4444 is an event.
- **Volume and direction.** Most hosts receive far more than they send.
  Inversion is the exfiltration signal.
- **Time of day.** Workstation traffic tracks working hours. Traffic that does
  not care what time it is was not generated by a person.
- **Flow duration and size distribution.** Human browsing is bursty and
  varied; automation is regular and uniform.

## Detections that need nothing but flow

- **Beaconing.** Regular intervals to one destination, from the previous path.
- **Data exfiltration.** Outbound volume far above this host's baseline, or a
  ratio inversion.
- **Scanning.** One source, many destinations, one port — or one destination,
  many ports. Both are trivial to count.
- **First contact.** This internal host has never spoken to this external
  address before, and neither has anyone else in the estate.
- **Lateral movement.** East-west flows that violate the segmentation policy,
  which also tells you your firewall rules are not doing what you think.
- **Dead infrastructure coming alive.** A host that has been silent for months
  starts talking.

## Working with it

```
# top talkers by bytes, last hour
nfdump -R /var/flows -t '2026-03-12 09:00:00-2026-03-12 10:00:00' \
       -s ip/bytes -n 20

# every flow involving a suspect address, ever
nfdump -R /var/flows 'host 45.83.201.14'

# who scanned: one source, many destinations
nfdump -R /var/flows -s srcip/flows -n 10 'proto tcp and flags S and not flags A'
```

The third one is the one to remember. A source with tens of thousands of
SYN-only flows is scanning, and it takes one query to find.

## Sampling, and reading it honestly

Most hardware samples — 1 in 1000 packets is common — because full flow
accounting at line rate is expensive. Consequences you must hold in mind:

- **Byte counts are estimates**, scaled by the sampling rate.
- **Small flows are frequently missed entirely.** A low-and-slow beacon may
  not appear at all.
- **Absence is not evidence.** "No flow record" means "not sampled" at least
  as often as it means "did not happen".

Know your sampling rate before you testify to a number, and say in the report
that the figure is sampled.

## Check yourself

- Name two investigative questions flow answers well and two it cannot answer
  at all.
- Why is a server contacting a new external address more significant than a
  workstation doing so?
- Your flow collector samples 1:1000. What does a *missing* record actually
  tell you?
